Using BIM efficiently will cut project time and thereby energy use and costs. BIM will reduce the waste of materials during construction and building management and eventually assist in sustainable demolition. Energy modeling can also minimize energy use over a building’s life

The ‘I’ is more important than the ‘B’ or ‘M’

Integrating multidisciplinary design inputs using a single 3D model allows interface issues to be identified and resolved in advance of construction, eliminating the cost and time impacts of a redesign. The model also enables new and existing assets to be integrated seamlessly. This will assist in creating exact quantity take-offs ensuring that materials are not over-ordered. Precise program scheduling enables just-in-time delivery of materials and equipment, reducing the potential for damage. The use of BIM for automated fabrication of equipment and components enables more efficient materials handling and waste recovery.

Better outcomes through collaboration

All project partners – different design disciplines, the customer, contractor, specialists and suppliers; will cooperate to develop a shared 3D model, cultivating collaborative working relationships. This ensures everyone is focused on achieving the best value, from project inception to eventual decommissioning.
Unfortunately, disciplines working separately, interacting only through the exchange of construction documents just won’t do anymore. BIM both enables and requires tighter integration. Both software and professionals must work closer together. But simply working together isn’t enough; habits and routines have to be aligned in order to make cooperation natural. The software will need to be developed to allow seamless integration, and so will the attitudes of professionals.
